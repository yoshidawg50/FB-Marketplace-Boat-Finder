# Tuna Feathers and Skirts

Feathers and soft skirts are the West Coast trolling staple — a weighted or
bullet head trailing a skirt over a hook, presenting as a small squid or bait.
They're surface lures that fish a **light-to-100 lb top-shot class** and catch
"just about everything that swims in our offshore waters." This note covers three
owned families — the **Williamson Tuna Catcher**, the **Williamson Live Series**
soft bait, and the **JAW Lures dual-skirt** — plus how to rig a bare tuna
feather.

## Running specs

**Williamson Tuna Catcher (rigged)**
- **Troll speed:** to 9 kt, best 6.5–8.
- **Running depth:** surface.
- **Setback:** 25–50 ft back.
- **Leader / top-shot:** factory 130 lb leader.

**Williamson Live Series soft bait**
- **Troll speed:** slow only, ~4–7 kt.
- **Running depth:** surface / subsurface.
- **Setback / role:** solo or as a frontline teaser.

**JAW Lures dual-skirt**
- **Troll speed:** real range 5–9 kt, best 6–8. **The "any speed" claim is
  marketing** — treat the 5–9 kt band as the working range.
- **Running depth:** surface.
- **Setback:** JAW's spec is 30–35 ft from the transom.
- **Leader / top-shot:** top-shot cap 100 lb (200 on the Offshore Dominator).

## How to run it

These are surface skirts — run them in the low single-digit-to-9 kt band close
enough that the head smokes and the skirt breathes (25–50 ft for the Tuna
Catcher, 30–35 ft for the JAW). The Live Series soft bait is a slow-only
(~4–7 kt) member that also works as a frontline teaser ahead of a hooked lure.
**When** to pull a skirt versus a swimmer, a cedar plug, or a speed-trolled
[Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) is decided in
[bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-trolling.md); general towing mechanics live
in [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md).

### Rigging a bare tuna feather

Full rig parameters and judgment live in
[tuna feather rig](../rigging/tuna-feather-rig.md); the visual steps are in the
source video, [How to rig a Tuna Feather (BDOutdoors)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CWVPLM6NheY).
In short: thread the leader through the head **first**, tie a barrel swivel to
the leader (any confident knot — improved clinch, San Diego, or uni), then pass
the hook point through the round end of that swivel and lock it with a small zip
tie so the hook swings free and self-clears fouls. **60–80 lb leader** is
preferred over the old 40 lb, which chafes and breaks off unnoticed. Slide the
head down onto the swivel and set the **bend of the hook roughly even with the
end of the skirt** so the skirt doesn't foul the point. Finish the other end of
the leader with the same knot.
