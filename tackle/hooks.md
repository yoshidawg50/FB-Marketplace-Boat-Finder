# Hooks — Style, Size, and Matching to Bait

A hook is chosen on three axes: **style** (how it sets), **size** (matched to the
bait, then the fish), and **wire gauge** (how much it burdens a live bait vs. how
much load it can take). Getting the bait to swim naturally and the hook into the
corner of the mouth is most of the game. Live-bait hooking mechanics are in
[fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md); this note is the selection
logic behind them.

## Style — how the hook sets

| Style | Shape | How it sets | Reach for it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Circle** | Point bent ~90° back toward the shank | **Don't swing.** Engage the reel, point the rod where the fish is going, wind tight — it pins the **corner of the mouth** on its own | Live bait when you can't or shouldn't set: sportboat crowds, clients who can't feel the bite, [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) / [yellowfin](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md), [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md) |
| **J hook** | Classic "J", point straight up | **Set it** — lift the rod tip, then wind tight | Reaction eaters and fish that run at the boat; [dorado](../species/dorado.md), island [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) |
| **Treble** | Three points | Self-hooking on the strike | Hard baits / plugs; the trailing hook on a flying-fish rig |
| **Assist** | Short cord + hook lashed to the jig's top | Hooks on the fall/lift | [Knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) and slow-pitch |

### Circle vs. J — the core decision (sWRSYCmt4Tw, m2q22sPPkEM)

- **Circle** is the default live-bait hook offshore because it does the work for
  you — the water's tension alone drives it into the jaw hinge, which is why
  long-liners and commercial rock-cod guys use them. Nose-hook the bait and just
  come tight. It's forgiving of anglers who never feel the bite.
- **J** wins where a circle can't get a good angle. **Dorado run straight at the
  boat**, killing the corner-of-mouth geometry a circle needs — switch to a J and
  set (sWRSYCmt4Tw). J hooks are also the pick for islands yellowtail, long-range,
  and [dropper-loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) work. For anglers who *can*
  feel the bite and like to set, a **long-shank J** (e.g. 7/0 for marlin) is
  favored on the theory that the long shank keeps the hook from being sawed off
  and lands more fish (m2q22sPPkEM — one captain's belief, single mention).

### Wire gauge

Same style, different wire, does different jobs (sWRSYCmt4Tw, m2q22sPPkEM):

- **Thin wire** for live bait. A thin-wire hook adds little weight to the bait's
  nose, so a sardine or anchovy swims naturally and draws more bites. Straightening
  a thin-wire hook is rare with a normal drag.
- **Heavy-duty (thick) wire** where strength beats swim — islands yellowtail,
  Guadalupe, dropper loop, and big-fish rigs. Thick wire on a small bait weighs it
  down and swims poorly, so save it for larger baits and heavier targets.

## Size — bait first, fish second

Hook numbering: below "1/0" the sizes count **up as they shrink** (1, 2 = small,
light); at and above **1/0** they count up as they **grow** (2/0, 4/0, 6/0 … up to
a big 11/0). The size is really the **gap** measured from the point across to the
shank, so it varies a bit between manufacturers (HIXTFWlwnM0).

**The bait sets the hook size; the target fish is secondary** (HIXTFWlwnM0,
m2q22sPPkEM):

| Bait | Typical hook | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchovy | **No. 4 thin-wire** (a small number-size, not an ought) | A big hook weighs the anchovy down and the fish see it; smaller = more eats. Historically San Clemente Island bluefin came on No. 4, even No. 8, thin wire — "just hook them" |
| Sardine (~5–7 in) | **2/0** for smaller-grade / picky tuna, up to **4/0–5/0** for mid-grade bluefin | 4/0 thin-wire is the classic sardine hook; nose-hook so it stays out of the bait's way and swims |
| Mackerel | Step up again — bigger hook | Bigger bait carries more hook |
| Live squid | **~6/0 thin-wire off an orange lead head**, or **4/0 with a sliding sinker** | See below |
| Size 1 / 2 | Very small, light duty | Scaling way down for finicky small-grade fish |
| 10/0–11/0 J + heavy treble | Flying-fish / kite rig | Line's out of the water, so hook size doesn't spook |

### Bluefin, first trip

For a first bluefin trip: **1/0 or 2/0 circle hooks, not J hooks** — you want the
hook set in the corner of the mouth (EyB98RuKfeQ), paired with a short fluoro
leader (see [line & leader](line-and-leader.md)).

### Live squid for white seabass (m2q22sPPkEM)

Rig squid on an **orange lead head with a ~6/0 thin-wire hook**, or a **4/0 hook
with a [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)**. Fish the **least
weight** that works — start light and drop to 1/8 or 1/4 oz — so the bait sinks
**slowly through the water column and along the kelp edge**.
[White seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) feed with the mouth out front, not on
the bottom; a bait lying on the bottom catches rays and shovelnose sharks instead.
A bullet weight slid down over the hook knot (point the bullet at the rod tip so
the hook eye seats up inside it) makes the hook nearly invisible.
