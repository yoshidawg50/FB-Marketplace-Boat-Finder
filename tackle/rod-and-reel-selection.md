# Rod & Reel Selection — by Application

The right stick is decided by the **application** (what you're throwing and at
what grade of fish), not by brand. This note maps SoCal applications —
flyline, surface iron, yo-yo, troll, jigging, bottom — onto **rod power + reel
class**, using the way experienced anglers actually build out their quiver. The
class shorthand (jig-stick class, 40–60 lb class, etc.) and its profile lookup
live in [gear classes](gear-classes.md); a full worked quiver is
[Cameron's 8 setups](../profiles/cameron/rods.md). For the trolling-specific
rod↔lure decision, use the **4-factor framework** in
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md).

## How the pieces map — application to rod power to reel

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Workhorse live bait | ~7 ft, 30 lb rated | 10-size 2-speed (Talica 10) | 65 lb braid | flyline class |
| Heavy live bait / sinker rig | 7 ft, H–XH | 800-size (Valiant 800) | 100 lb braid | [yo-yo](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) / sinker |
| Casting poppers / stickbaits | ~8 ft, stiff | Stella 5000 spin **or** conventional | 65 lb braid | reaction-bite class |
| Surface iron | 8–10 ft jig stick, moderate | narrow high-speed conventional | 40–65 lb braid | [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) |

## The offshore quiver — a top-5 framework (xPFm_ZV2PZU)

A practical San Diego offshore spread runs **lightest to heaviest**, five sticks
covering every scenario. Build yours around these roles:

1. **Paddy setup** (goes every trip): ~8 ft rod rated ~20 lb, flexy; turn-400-size
   reel; 50 lb braid backing + ~25 yds mono; 15 lb Gold Label fluoro leader (~5 ft)
   to a small 1/0 J hook. For paddy [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) and
   [dorado](../species/dorado.md).
2. **Workhorse live bait** (every trip; doubles as rock cod): 7 ft, 30 lb-rated
   rod; Talica-10-size reel; 65 lb braid; leader ≈ rod length (7–8 ft) of 30 lb;
   2/0 live-bait circle. Smaller [yellowfin](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md) (15–25 lb)
   and smaller [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).
3. **Heavy live bait / sinker rig** (overnight, multi-day): 7 ft heavy/extra-heavy
   rod; 800-size reel; 100 lb braid; long leader (~15 ft) of 80 lb (80–100 lb) blue
   label. Sardine sinker rigs and the nighttime flat-fall rig.
4. **Casting setup:** 8 ft rod; a modern spinning reel (Stella 5000) *or*
   conventional; 65 lb braid; short heavier leader (~4–5 ft) of 60–100 lb blue
   label. Stick baits and poppers. (For surface iron, a jig stick is still more
   fun — see below.)
5. **Big tuna:** rail rod (2x4) / 50–100 lb; HD30-size 2-speed; 130 lb braid; a
   Bimini or splice to a [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md), 150 lb
   blue label (~20–25 ft), crimped to a snap swivel. Kite flyer rigs step to
   300 lb line with a big 10/0–11/0 J and an extra-heavy treble.

The lesson isn't the exact rods — it's the **ladder of line classes** and having a
dedicated tool at each rung so you're not under- or over-gunned when the grade
shows up.

## Sportboat: bring a range, day and night

On a charter you can't pick the grade in advance, so carry a spread
(daytime/nighttime roundups, gOAOyMNG3Ug, qSgIwLX2FWw). Grade can swing from
30–50 lb fish to 100–200 lb fish on the same trip.

- **Daytime:** a knife-jigging rod (PE 4x8, 250–400 g; 100 lb braid, day size-down
  to a 60–80 lb fluoro top shot; **100–200 g jigs by day**); a favorite flyline
  rod (25–40 lb, 30 lb braid, 25–30 lb fluoro); a whippier **paddy** flyline rod to
  pitch small baits; a stiff **popper** rod (9 ft); and a 15–40 lb do-everything rod
  that also casts a 60–80 g Colt Sniper-style jig at finicky bluefin.
- **Nighttime:** the same jigging rod but **200–400 g jigs**; a 20–40 lb **gray-light**
  transition rod (65 lb braid, 40 lb Gold Label top shot, size-2 circle hooks); and
  two big rods — a 50–100 lb (Calstar 770H class, Fathom 60, 130 lb braid,
  100–130 lb top shot) that also fishes a daytime sinker rig, plus a 7'6" XXH
  50–100 lb with a parabolic tip that presents a jig better. **Never fish under
  100 lb line at night.**

## Inshore baitcast — three sizes (Academy Ep15, ONH1K2MOp7Q)

Low-profile saltwater baitcasters were designed for largemouth, so **avoid the
high-speed gear ratios** bass anglers use (8:1+): a SoCal gamefish doesn't swim at
you, so a fast reel just piles torque on you and makes crankbaits miserable to
wind. Landesfeind learned this getting worked by a ~60 lb bluefin on an ~8:1 400.
Three classic sizes cover inshore:

- **200 size (~6.6:1)** — the workhorse. Brim-full of 30 lb braid. Crankbaits,
  smaller hard baits, [swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md) for
  [spotties](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) /
  [calicos](../species/calico-bass.md) / [sandbass](../species/sand-bass.md),
  5-in baits on ≤1 oz heads. Leader by lure: 30–40 lb for cranks, ~15 lb for small
  swimbaits, straight-tie or 20 lb for hard baits. 30 lb braid is thin and prone to
  backlash over a day of casting — carry a spool aboard to strip and refill. A
  second 200 can be dedicated to **fluoro line** (15 lb for small jerkbaits, 10 lb
  for a fluke on a ball head), filled halfway with 30 lb braid then topped with
  fluoro.
- **300 size (~7.3:1)** — heavier line. 50 lb braid for sandbass, shallow island
  rockfish, or a slug on a lead head for suspended calicos; 65 lb on a dedicated
  weedless rod (it won't hold much 65, but casts the big weedless better).
- **400 size (~6.2:1)** — bigger body/handle/drags, more line per crank. 65 lb
  braid brim-full on a ~9 ft blank makes a one-rod island setup: big hard baits,
  weedless, even a lure to sub-80 lb tuna, or 30 lb braid for artificial rockfish
  at 400–500 ft. These bass rods have landed bluefin to ~80 lb (300 size) and a
  ~150 lb class fish to color (400 size).

Buy **saltwater-series** reels over freshwater models — they cost more but outlast
them by a long shot.

## Inshore / shoreline all-in-one combo (8tTVMOV2arE)

A single versatile combo (e.g. a DC-braked baitcaster with birdnest-reducing
presets on an 8 ft medium, extra-fast inshore rod) covers spotties, calicos, and
halibut from boat or beach, and has the backbone for 20–30 lb yellowfin in a
pinch. Line: ~150 yds braid + 3–5 ft fluoro top shot — **8–10 lb for spotties,
12–15 lb for [halibut](../species/california-halibut.md), 20–30 lb for calicos in
the kelp** (they dig in and you need to pull them out).

## Trolling rods — use the 4-factor framework

Trolling rod choice is its own decision because the lure, not the fish, sets the
load. Match rod to lure by **tow/drag load, line-abrasion needs, strike
cushioning, and line-agnostic cases** — the full logic and the rod-tip elevation
rule are in [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md); per-lure specs live in the lure
notes.

## Resolving a class to a real rod

With no profile, a plan names the **class** ("surface-iron class, 40–60 lb"). With
a profile it resolves to the owned stick — see
[Cameron's 8 setups](../profiles/cameron/rods.md), where every rod is tagged with
its class, braid weight, and role (and every one but the trolling reel has caught
a bluefin).
