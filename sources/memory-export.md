## 2. Personal doctrine & on-water observations

- **Foamer program (fall 2025 late-season bluefin, recalled 7/26/26):** epic finish to the season within panga range. Ran NO bait — couldn't hold enough of the only available bait (big sardines) to chum effectively, and skipping the 30-gal tank cut water weight on high-mileage run-and-gun days. ~2–3 casts per school before finding a new one. Most fish caught on Colt Snipers, many actually snagged — smaller presentation was better. Fish landed were 25–40 lb; saw 80–140 lb foamer schools but never hooked one.
- **Foamer technique:** run at working terns fast, set up ahead of the school's direction of travel, stop hard (25 kt straight to neutral), cast immediately as close to the middle of the foam as possible. Long casting distance very helpful. A backlash on the arrival cast kills your shot at that school.
- **Slack tide model:** plans around slack tide for tuna — believes foamers are most likely to start at slack because slack lets bait rise in the water column.
- **Water color doctrine:** in warm-water years he weights chlorophyll/water color at least as heavily as SST when picking water — won't fish "dirty green" water for gamefish.
- **SD Bay entrance swell exposure (his own chart work):** open to swell from roughly 160°–186° (measured channel seaward axis 186°); Baja blocks east of ~160°, Point Loma blocks west of the channel axis.
- **Modeling stance (7/27/26):** for yellowtail, the calendar date/month doesn't matter — they follow water temperature and bait, and the bait follows temperature and currents; calendar patterns in his catch data are downstream of water state. (Known tension with the BD corpus's year-anniversary prior for bluefin routes — keep both, attributed.)
- **Turnover doctrine (BightSST work):** fishing is best right before turnover — the sharp SST drop + chlorophyll spike; time-to-turnover / the approach is the key model output, not the turnover event itself.
- **Fleet intelligence practices:** tracks sportboat AIS stop/start patterns at the Coronados (e.g., the Grande, MMSI 366746210) and rates it among the most valuable data for learning the islands; wants stops correlated against tide, wind, SST, chlorophyll, and current. Marks Everingham's bait boats on MarineTraffic to see when the SD Bay/Mission Bay receivers get refilled during bait shortages. Monitors VHF ch 72 offshore on busy weekends — hears Navy ship-to-ship traffic, same ships/operators recurring week to week.
- **Data capture:** boat chartplotter is a Garmin GPSMAP 840xs; wants track line data exported for analysis. Built a "Format Photo Coordinates" iOS shortcut — share-sheet action that copies a photo's GPS position (degrees/decimal-minutes) + timestamp for marking spots; confirmed working 7/27/26.

## 3. Bird-reading model (his current model, stated 8/3/26)

- **Terns:** his best bird — will almost always stop and cast on working terns; offshore he'll follow terns around to find fish even with no other birds working. Strong on tern reads; actively building the same read for gulls, pelicans, cormorants, shearwaters, and other SD seabirds.
- **Gulls:** "good sign but dumb" — general measure of life in an area, and paddy finders.
- **Pelicans:** "dirty birds" — diving pelicans usually mean he's in the wrong area; casting there typically produces mackerel.
- **Boobies:** mostly sees them at the Coronados; considers them worth paying attention to.
- **Cormorants:** previously thought of them only as bait stealers; open to using them as a bait-depth indicator.
- **Shearwaters:** doesn't currently factor them in. (The BD corpus has a full shearwater doctrine — merge attributed; this is exactly the gap his model wants filled.)

- Rod↔lure pairing framework (8/10/26), four factors: (1) tow/drag load requiring heavy rods (e.g., full-size Madmacs at speed-troll), (2) swimmers whose action dies on heavy line, (3) lures needing a softer rod to absorb the strike, (4) line-agnostic lures where the rod just matches the target species.
