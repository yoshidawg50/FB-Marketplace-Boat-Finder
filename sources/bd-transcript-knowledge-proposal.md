## §1 — Sea-state interpretation layer (NEW)

The skill now pulls per-spot and per-route wind + swell (8/7/26 requirement). These rules turn that raw pull into judgment. All from Academy Ep. 6 (marine forecasts) unless noted.

1. **Steepness rule.** Swell period matters more than height. 10 ft @ 15 s from the south is barely felt in open water; 3–4 ft @ 4–5 s is brutal. Flag any zone/route segment where period (seconds) approaches height (feet).
2. **Current-vs-swell opposition.** Summer uphill (counter)current running against NW swell compresses the interval and stands the swell up — a 4 ft @ 10 s forecast fishes like 6 ft @ 7–8 s. Windy/NWS won't show this. The skill already pulls HF radar + WCOFS currents: compute opposition (current vector vs swell direction) along the route and degrade the forecast where they oppose.
3. **Bathymetric slop hotspots.** Where current accelerates over a ridge/shoal, swell stands up locally (his example: the ridge off the east end of Catalina — horrible for a few miles, lays down 3–4 mi past it). Mark bathymetric features on the transit where the current field accelerates; annotate "expect locally worse seas here; usually improves beyond" instead of letting one rough patch imply the whole outer zone is blown.
4. **Wind history, not just wind forecast.** A calm-morning forecast hides overnight wind — he got beat up by leftover chop from a south wind that blew all night. Conception-area corollary: NW blows at *night* up there and stops by morning, so a calm 6 a.m. reading is a trap; he wants 2+ consecutive days of no-wind or Santa Anas before a Rosa/Miguel crossing (Ep. 6; 11/23/22 report). → Add a 12–24 h wind *hindcast* for the route and zones to every conditions pull.
5. **Upstream fetch check.** Swell arrives later than the wind that made it. His San Nicolas example: calm locally all weekend, 4 ft → 12 ft swell by Monday from a system off Oregon days earlier. Scan the synoptic wind field up-coast over the prior 48–72 h for swell-arrival risk the point forecast underexplains.
6. **Unforecast-wind heuristics.** (a) Extreme inland heat (100 °F+) can break down into unforecast afternoon W wind on the water; if it's windier than forecast in the morning, assume it builds all day — small boat bails early (7/14/22). (b) Forecast trust is asymmetric: assume it errs worse, and afternoon W at the islands strengthens (Ep. 6). (c) The Catalina Eddy can shield the inner SD banks/tuna grounds while it blows outside — check eddy state when the outer forecast looks bad (5/26/22).
7. **Santa Ana = northern-islands window.** Santa Ana events flatten the NW outer islands: the trigger to run Rosa/San Miguel for shallow rockfish/lings (10/20, 11/23/22).
8. **Homebound wind.** Runs south into Mexican paddy water: W wind on the return = uphill misery; plan return timing against the afternoon W build (8/17/22).

## §2 — Moon interpretation layer (UPGRADE)

The skill pulls moon phase (July 2026) but has no rules attached. Repeated, dated observations across the 2022 cycle:

1. **Bite windows.** Days *leading into* full moon are the strongest documented bluefin windows (10/12/22: "if we've learned anything over the last six, seven years"); WSB bites at the islands cluster on the moons in season — full (7/14, 8/11/22) and new (8/31/22, 22 fish); late-Nov bluefin day windows tied to the new moon (11/23/22); sword bites rise around both new and full and the weeks flanking them (seminar). One dissenting sportboat captain (2020 short): best is 7 days before AND 7 days after, with the moon itself the travel period. Reconciled rule: treat full/new ±week as elevated-probability windows, with the leading days strongest and the moon peak itself a *relocation* event.
2. **Post-moon relocation ≠ disappearance.** On the moon, fish travel at night (bright-night confidence) and schools scatter/relocate; open-water bluefin can move 10–15 nm overnight (7/21/22). After the Oct full moon, "the tuna are gone" was a coverage illusion — the fleet lost them and stopped reporting (10/12/22). Skill rules: post-moon, (a) heavily discount report persistence, (b) treat *absence of reports* as coverage bias rather than absence of fish, (c) widen the search prior toward same-date prior-year locations (see §5.9).
3. **Daytime bass suppression.** Full moon (clear nights): calico/sand bass gorge at night and shut down by mid-morning — bite early then dead until late day (7/21, 8/11, 10/12/22); winter version = reaction-bite-only days (12/15/22). Plan bass segments for first light or afternoon on full moons.
4. **Kelp paddies restock at night.** Fish move paddy-to-paddy in darkness only; a paddy that's dry stays dry all day — check fast (look, sonar, one bait) and move; plan a paddy *line*, not a stakeout ("5 Tips" short). Moon-bright nights = bigger redistribution.
5. **Tide amplitude coupling.** Springs on full/new, neaps on quarters (Ep. 2) — feeds §3 and the existing tidal-phase pull.
6. **Arrival events.** New waves of bluefin observed arriving "out of the west on the moon" into the Ensenada zone (7/21/22) — moons as turnover markers, not just bite markers.

## §3 — Tide / slack timing layer (UPGRADE)

The convergence layer pulls tidal phase; these are the decision rules to attach. Consistent with Cameron's own slack-tide foamer model.

1. **Arrival discipline.** Be at the highest-percentage zone **an hour before** the tide change (2/20/25). The late-morning slack (when one falls after ~9:30) is the prime open-water pop-up window; pop-ups also cluster in the afternoon — re-run water you marked earlier in the day at those times (7/14, 10/26/22).
2. **Window compression.** When the bite is off-peak (post-moon, pressured, marginal conditions), action compresses into the tide windows; wide-open days bite all day (8/17/22). So: strong conditions → route freely; weak conditions → the plan should anchor zones to tide times.
3. **Species tide-dependence.** Calico = current-driven, tide-indifferent; harbor spotties = fully tide-driven; WSB, marlin, and bluefin foamers = slack-tide biters (2/20/25; Decker leaving sign before slack and regretting it, 9/14/22). Slack mechanism offered: the water column "goes quiet," bait rises, predators hunt better — same as Cameron's bait-rise model, independently stated.
4. **WSB/halibut water triad.** "Seabass-color" milky water alone is not a spot. Needs all three: off-color patch + current + bait present. Missing any → leave within 20–30 min (Ep. 15). The productive version is the *local variation*: a dirty-water patch with a hard rip/foam edge inside otherwise cleaner water — fish hide in the dirt to ambush (2/20/25; Ep. 15).

## §4 — Current-structure doctrine (NEW — the largest conceptual addition)

This encodes exactly what the standing preference asks for: reasoning about how water moves across structure rather than naming spots. Sources: Ep. 1, Ep. 5, Ep. 14, 2/20/25, 8/31/22.

1. **Mechanism chain with a time lag.** Current + structure → upwelling ("thumb over the hose") → nutrients + light → phytoplankton (the chlorophyll signal) → zooplankton → baitfish and red crab → gamefish. Fresh upwelled water is cold, green, and *bad now but productive later* — an overnight ~5 °F drop + green water after afternoon NW wind is a local upwelling event: short-term negative, mark it for the recovery (Ep. 1). This gives the skill's chlorophyll pull an age dimension: distinguish new cold bloom (avoid) from mature bloom edges (fish).
2. **Where on the bank.** With summer uphill current, upwelling forms where the current impinges on the bank — the lower/south face ("that uphill current hits the lower end of those banks," Ep. 1). Within-bank spot selection: bias toward the up-current face under the prevailing current vector the skill already pulls.
3. **Parallel-current rule (coastal/island structure).** Current parallel to the shoreline (uphill or downhill) = good — it creates a definable leading edge and makes land-attached reef pinch-points work. Straight into the beach = marginal, sometimes bites. Straight off the beach = dead, avoid (Ep. 14 states all three explicitly; 2/20/25 gives the mechanism). Spot typology: land-attached perpendicular ridges are two-directional spots (parallel only); standalone banks (Farnsworth type) fish in any direction but each has preferred directions; coastal deep spots default-prefer downhill (N→S) (2/20/25).
4. **Spatial coherence of current.** If spot A ran direction X on a day a month ago and spot C/D matched it that day, they'll likely match again (Ep. 14). One current observation propagates across the day's spot list — cheap inference the skill can make from a single HF-radar cell or one on-water report.
5. **Current-strength diagnostics from observables.** Kelp pulled 20 ft under = raging current (surface presentations dead); kelp laid down = strong; kelp resurfacing = slacking, switch presentation back (8/31/22; Ep. 14). Chum-drift angle vs boat lay diagnoses true current vs wind (2/20/25). No current at anchor = bait pointing all directions, fish metering but not biting — "the treadmill" is off (2/20/25).
6. **Fathom-curve behavior at islands.** Where an island rises off the plain (Catalina's ~500-fm curve), uphill current compresses, spins eddies, and aggregates bait *and* kelp paddies along the curve. The 8/31/22 worked example is the template: one popped fish near the curve → identify the structure it related to → run the curve slowly and wait, don't sprint to where the fleet went; return to your own marked positions at tide time. Encode as: "sign associated with structure = repeatable decision; sign in no-man's-land = low value" (his words, nearly verbatim).
7. **Paddy geometry.** Open-water paddies align N–S under summer uphill current — find one, search its N–S line; near a bank edge they align along the contour — follow the contour, not the compass (7/14/22). Boats parked on a paddy → look up/down its line rather than crashing it (8/11/22). Paddy pressure gradient: the closer to a dock and the more traffic, the less willing the fish; paddies scarcer and less holding north of Dana Point — best paddy water south of SCI/Cat and Dana-to-Oceanside southward (8/17/22).
8. **Bait-shape reading.** A solid unbroken wall of bait on the meter usually has no predators on it; broken, harried, or moving bait does (Ep. 5). Bait was holding in the *dirtier* water while clean blue water was empty — clean-water trolling found nothing because the bait wasn't there (8/17/22). Predators work the dirty/clean edge (Duane: bluefin transit the chalky transition; the dirty side holds the bait).
9. **Deep scattering layer as a conditions object.** The DSL (600+ ft by day, rising at night) is the reason night bluefin and daytime sword work; see §7. Worth first-class status in the skill's vocabulary alongside SST/chlorophyll/current.

## §5 — Seasonal priors calendar (NEW reference)

Month-by-month expectations from the 2022 cycle + winter 2025. Proposed as a reference file the skill consults when weighting zones — priors, never current intel (the 7-day report cutoff stands; this is the pattern layer those stale reports are allowed to feed).

1. **Feb–Mar:** Winter bass logic — kelp / deep structure / shallow taxonomy; sand bass on deep structure; crankbait on suspended bait balls over pipes works all winter. Multi-day rain puts fishing "in a funk"; runoff washes out island bass zones (Catalina's dirt hillsides especially) while deep structure stays fishable — post-rain plan = deep structure or harbor riprap (2/6/25). Velella (by-the-wind sailor) events can make bass bites unconvertible (2/27/25). Bait-size regime check: anchovy/"rice" cycles → downsize everything; match lure size to the bait *in the zone*, not the tank (2/27/25).
2. **April:** Bluefin still far south, sonar-school + night-jig fishery, mostly out of skiff range — sportboat advantage is being in the zone before gray light. Wind-rollover geography: a big NW blow crashes the northern Channel Islands and the LA/Ventura coastal shelf 5–8 °F; south of Newport is "less prone to wind-wave-driven rollovers"; Coronados/tuna grounds/Cat/SCI held through it (4/21/22). Coronado yellows run "seeing-not-biting" cycles with narrow daily windows. SCI squid-zone bites die when the squid moves deep — fish didn't leave, relocation needed.
3. **May:** Bluefin creep into US waters ahead of the volume; first paddy yellows appear with them; night bite + daytime flying-fish imitations for grade; run-and-gun casting opens for small boats. Tuna group by size class (≤60 / 60–80 / 80–100+): seeing the 80+ class changes presentation (5/26/22).
4. **June–July:** Full moons drive island WSB and the bluefin night bite (§2). July bluefin geography: the below-Clemente complex — 43, 181, 182, 289, Clemente Ridge up to Mackerel Bank — reads as **one ~50–60 sq mi search box** (plan hours of looking, not a spot-check); contrast with compact spots (302, 371, west-of-Cat) (7/21/22). Coastal upwelling is patchy — cold/dirty inshore water is a *relocate-one-mile* problem tied to a break or ridge, whereas an offshore rollover is basin-wide and must be fished through (7/14/22).
5. **August:** Peak dispersal — bluefin from Cat to Nick to Ventura, "showing up in weird places, gone the next day"; whole fleet shifts offshore, islands lose coverage. Paddy program peaks (see §4.7 for the pressure gradient). Yellowfin arrive; they're more lure-cooperative and a surface yellowfin bite can fire the bluefin (8/3/22). Foamer taxonomy: "jacuzzi" (solid whitewater) ≈ bite every cast; spread-out directional feeders = picky — wait for the circular form (8/17, 8/31/22). Tuna aren't outrunning you: they move low single-digit miles per hour — troll sweeping arcs around the bird/bait area instead of straight-lining away, and don't troll through the fleet's prop wash (8/17/22).
6. **September:** The "volume square" — SBI–Nick–Clemente–Cat — holds the bigger bluefin; dorado in open-water schools below Cat; yellowfin volume near the border sliding north with the warm water, and the bluefin slide with them. Bluefin observed foaming in **76 °F** — above the usual assumed ceiling; treat warm SST as soft evidence against bluefin, not hard (9/14/22). Hurricane remnants: usually a non-event that breaks the heat, fishing unaffected (9/14/22).
7. **October:** Regime change — "conditions become pretty important… look 100% at your temp chart and chlorophyll." Bluefin sit the **cool side** of a break when the water is warm (warm side when it's cold); yellowfin want the **warmest + cleanest** combination (SST max × chlorophyll min) (10/12, 10/20/22). The warm band tracks: backside Santa Cruz/Rosa → SBI → backside Cat → hooks to the 499/Clemente; as the coast cools and fragments, bluefin condense outward to Tanner/Cortez. Everything season (marlin, first swords, BFT, YFT, dorado) but scattered. Grunion-in-kelp events override water quality entirely — every-cast bass in ugly stagnant water (10/20/22). Marlin-fleet tell: outriggers + circling = tournament, not tuna (9/14/22).
8. **November–December:** Santa Anas open Rosa/Miguel (§1.7); lings shallow — the target window; Tanner/Cortez bluefin continue night-dominant with 300 lb fish through Nov, then revert to the sub-30 lb winter grade by mid-Dec; first sword flags mid-Dec; rockfish closes year-end. Late-fall island bass wanted off-color water + current **into** the kelp at SCI (11/23/22 — see §10.1). Sharp offshore edges reappear (63 → 61.5 °F in ~1 mi, 8 mi off SCI) with sign on the edge but deep, sonar-dependent fish.
9. **Year-anniversary prior.** "Go back to this date last year and the year before — pretty close to where they'll be" (10/12/22); Duane's version: line up the prior days' catch positions and project the travel line (2020). The bluefin route repeats year over year — worth encoding as a same-week historical prior the skill consults, strongest in fall.

## §6 — Search & bird doctrine (NEW — plan-execution layer)

For the skill's plan output ("how to run the day"), and directly feeding the seabird model in /topics/birding.md.

1. **Glassing protocol** (7/14/22, dorado video): stabilized binos; full stop (or bow into swell, ~1 kt); 360° in small increments, 3–4 min per scan; watch any bird 30–40 s before judging. Effective ranges: a few terns ~0.5–1 mi from deck level, a big bird spot ~2 mi; from a hardtop, birds ~2 mi and dolphins ~4 mi. Station spacing 3–4 mi. Along a bank: run the contour at 16–18 mph with a lookout ~30° off each bow. On a current break: it's a hard edge, so run fast between glass stops. Gray-calm mornings: scan *down-swell* a quarter-mile out and watch paddies roll over the swell crests. "If you're not burning batteries every trip or two, you're not looking enough."
2. **Terns:** best bird. Locked-in repeated dives on one spot = bait only; a *moving* dive-line = bait being pushed by fish underneath (7/21/22).
3. **Shearwaters:** rafted = sitting over deep bait waiting for the tide. Drive-through test: circle straight back = fish under them; scatter and land elsewhere = bait only. Rafts hopscotching 20–30 ft at a time = tracking deep fish. When shearwaters race off low in one direction from a boil, follow them — they run to the *head* of the school; the old boil is the tail (7/21/22).
4. **Gulls:** unreliable (parking-lot birds); only meaningful locked-in at islands/kelp edges with nothing better around. **Cormorants:** diving *in kelp* = actively feeding = bass indicator, better than gulls inshore. **Pelicans:** worthless as gamefish indicators. **Mixed piles** (gulls+pelicans+terns+cormorants) at open-water current breaks = bait only, skip; the exception is a mixed pile *on* kelp/structure — that can be a bait event (the grunion blitz, 10/20/22) and is worth a look.
5. **Birds sitting on the water still mark the zone** — trolling past sitting birds and hopscotching rafts produced immediate bites when nothing was flying (8/17/22). Riprap version: birds sitting on the rocks mark the thumb-over-hose deviation (Ep. 9).
6. **Bird radar** (Duane): gain near max, range 4–5 mi; bird schools paint as red blotches, sometimes boat-sized. Directly relevant to the T-top + bird-mode radar idea — 4–5 mi is the working detection envelope to expect.
7. **Anchoring geometry** (2/20/25): bottom fish → anchor on the spot; midwater/gamefish → anchor up-current of it, standoff scaled to current strength; break-off risk (yellows) → further still. Chum bucket = artificial thumb-over-hose; its drift line is the current sensor.
8. **Sportboat bow play** (7/14/22): anchored at SCI, chum gets pushed *up-current* toward the bow by fish — watch for birds locking on there and put the surface iron under them.

## §7 — Swordfish module (NEW program)

Cameron is researching SoCal daytime swordfish (manual reels). The Feb 2021 seminar (Duane Diego Mellor + Rush Maltz + Ali Hussainy) is essentially the module spec:

1. **Season:** late September – December; peak Oct–mid Nov (year-dependent: one year hot late Sept/Oct, the next mid-Nov, last fish ~a week before Christmas). Holdover fish year-round but generally not worth the fuel Jan–Aug.
2. **Weather ceiling:** fair-weather technique. >15 kt = no-go; comfortable ceiling 10–13 kt; drift ≤ ~2–2.5 kt for presentation; leads 7–10.5 lb matched to drift; drift sock extends marginal days. Preferred drift: "down and in" along the contour edges.
3. **Where:** 150–350 fm on contour lines, thumbs, notches, humps — named working spots: the Ridge, the 9, the 43, the 178, the 302, the 138/182 area on break days. West Coast model: set up and let the fish come along the fathom-curve highways.
4. **The DSL is the key.** Sat-tag work (PIER/Sepúlveda) showed the fish ride the deep scattering layer by day. Requirements before stopping: DSL present at ≥600 ft (prefer the 900 ft–150 fm band); DSL **plus** chunky bait balls (anchovy/hake/squid) at 100–150+ fm ≈ bites near-assured. Fish the bait *in* the DSL and 100–250 ft below it; baits average 800–1,100 ft, shallower when the layer is shallow; DSL at 850 → bait at 900.
5. **Water:** SST + chlorophyll breaks = converging masses; prefer one big powerful "highway" break over finger rips; find the biggest rip in the zone; **structure × current-edge intersection = the banger days** — "you could have been there a week earlier and couldn't buy a bite."
6. **Moon:** bites rise on new and full and the flanking weeks (§2.1).
7. **Sounder for DSL:** TVG/gain pushed up (Furuno ~55–57; mid-high on other brands), ping speed slowed (~15 of 20) to paint the layer. Translates to the Garmin 840xs as: gain up, slower ping, watch for the band.
8. **Diet corroboration:** stomachs full of hake, small rockfish, squid — bottom-adjacent feeders; one Catalina fish full of mullet (they range inshore at night).
9. **Bite handling:** the best hookups came from rods nobody touched — let them wolf it (buoy-gear logic); ~7–9 min to deploy two baits; set the drift up-current of the fathom line so the bait sweeps the structure.

## §8 — Parameters for layers the skill already has

Not new machinery — numbers and rules that slot into existing components:

1. **Advection model:** open-water bluefin not holding structure move **10–15 nm overnight** (7/21/22); intraday they move low single-digit mph (8/17/22). Use as the displacement prior in the 7/23 forecasting layer.
2. **Persistence-null:** post-moon relocation (§2.2) is a named trigger for the existing persistence-null check; add "absence of reports after a moon or late-season = coverage bias" to source-language discipline (10/12, 10/26/22).
3. **Temp bands:** bluefin surface-feeding observed at 76 °F (9/14/22) — widen the bluefin ceiling from "hard" to "soft" evidence. Break-side rule: bluefin cool side in warm regimes, warm side in cold regimes (10/20/22); yellowfin = warmest × cleanest (10/20/22); dorado concentration at the green-cold / blue-warm coastal boundary, ~71–72 °F reference (dorado video).
4. **Chlorophyll age:** new upwelling (cold+green, post-wind) vs mature bloom edge — §4.1. The skill pulls chlorophyll every run; add the freshness interpretation.
5. **Report reading:** "conditions rolled over — don't rely on your pre-fish" after storms (Ep. 15) — post-weather-event pattern volatility belongs beside water-miles report aging.
6. **Wind/swell pull additions:** 12–24 h wind hindcast per route/zone (§1.4); current-vs-swell opposition computation (§1.2); steepness flag (§1.1).

## §9 — Out of scope for the skill (belongs elsewhere)

Extracted but proposed for memory/tackle files, not the skill:

- **SPJ/jig rules** (→ jigging-rod project): slow-pitch weight ≈ 1.5 g per foot of depth; speed jig ≈ 1 g/ft; high current or wind → longer/slender jig, slack → fatter/fluttery; thin braid (PE1.5–2 class on small rigs; 80 lb sinks faster than 100 on tuna gear) so jigs fall straight — jumping to 40–50 lb braid adds scope; BOLA yellowtail SPJ zone 150–300 ft on high spots, 130–250 g wing-falls ate well; night bluefin: mark line every 100 ft (or color-metered braid), throw up-current at the bow and walk back to stay vertical, single bottom assist pair (no top hook), 2 ft of 200 lb bite leader; jig weight/night depth called by captain 150–400 g.
- **Fish care** (→ fishing/food memory if wanted): rock salt (~2 cups per 7–10 lb bag) + splash of seawater = below-freezing slurry; field-dress tuna (guts+gills), wet-towel cooldown before icing; chill times 30–40 lb ≈ 2 h, ~100 lb ≈ 3–5 h, >100 lb overnight re-iced; dorado: no bleed, ice immediately, never freshwater-rinse, bring a car cooler for the drive home.
- **Electronics:** Furuno bottom-discrimination "highways" where hard meets soft bottom; photograph the meter during bites to learn marks; stop only on marks you can grade (bait budget).
- **Technique-selection links** the skill could cite in plan output: kite when wind allows, speed-troll (13–15 kt, Mad Mac class) to locate when it doesn't; dorado wake-up tricks (deck-hose spray, slow-trolled sardine swung into the paddy, chunking).

## §10 — Flagged conflicts / uncertainties

1. **Current into the beach.** Ep. 14 and 2/20/25: parallel best, into-beach marginal, off-beach dead. But 11/23/22 at SCI the bites required current running *into* the island with off-color water. Proposal: encode parallel as the prior, into-beach as a viable late-fall/off-color state, off-beach as dead. (Same author, both statements — this is a real seasonal nuance, not an error.)
2. **Moon lore.** The lunar claims are captain observation, not measurement, and the two sources weight the windows differently (§2.1). Encoded as probability adjustments, not gates.
3. **Post-rain pressure.** He was explicitly unsure whether lingering low pressure beats post-frontal high pressure for winter fishing (2/6/25). Not proposed for encoding.
4. **76 °F bluefin** is a single observation flagged as unusual by the observer himself. Softens the ceiling; doesn't move the band.
5. All of this is 2020–2025 material — pattern-layer only. The skill's hard 7-day report cutoff is untouched; nothing here is current intel.
