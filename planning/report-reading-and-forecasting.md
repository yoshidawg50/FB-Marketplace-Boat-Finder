# Report Reading and Forecasting

How to age, discount, and project fishing reports into a plan. Reports are the
pattern layer; live [BightSST](day-plan-protocol.md) conditions are the current
layer. This note is about turning yesterday's information into today's expected
position — and knowing when *not* to trust it.

## Advection — reports have a shelf life measured in miles

Fish move between when a report is made and when you get there. Displace the
report before you use it:

- **Open-water bluefin not holding structure move 10–15 nm overnight**
  (7/21/22). A gray-light plan built on yesterday's coordinates is already
  10–15 nm stale.
- **Intraday they move at low single-digit miles per hour** (8/17/22). Within a
  day the school drifts; the pop-up you marked at 8 a.m. is not at the same GPS
  point at noon.

Use these as the displacement prior: draw a box around a reported position sized
by how old the report is, not a pin. Combine with the
[year-anniversary prior](../seasonal/year-anniversary-prior.md) to project the
travel line.

## Post-moon relocation ≠ disappearance

On and just after the moon, fish travel at night and schools scatter and
relocate (bright-night confidence) — open-water bluefin can move 10–15 nm
overnight (7/21/22). After the October full moon, "the tuna are gone" was a
**coverage illusion**: the fleet lost them and stopped reporting (10/12/22). See
[moon](../conditions/moon.md) for the full lunar layer. Rules for the days after
a moon:

- **Heavily discount report persistence.**
- **Widen the search prior** toward same-date prior-year locations.
- Treat the moon peak itself as a **relocation event**, not a bite-death.

## Persistence-null — absence of reports is not absence of fish

**Post-moon and late-season, "absence of reports = coverage bias," not absence
of fish** (10/12, 10/26/22). This is the persistence-null trigger:

- When the fleet shifts offshore or thins out (August dispersal, late-season,
  post-moon), you simply stop *hearing* about water that is still holding fish.
- Do not read a quiet zone as a dead zone when the quiet is explained by where
  the boats went. Ask *why* there are no reports before concluding there are no
  fish.

## Post-storm volatility — don't rely on your pre-fish

**"Conditions rolled over — don't rely on your pre-fish"** after storms
(SoCal Bight Fishing Academy Ep. 15). Post-weather-event pattern volatility sits
right beside water-miles report aging: a storm can reset the water so that
scouting done before it is worthless. After any significant weather event,
re-scout — treat pre-storm intel as expired regardless of how few days old it
is. See [sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md) and
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md).

## Source-language discipline

Normalize what a report actually says before you weight it:

- **Normalize counts per rod.** "50 fish" means nothing without the number of
  anglers — convert to fish per rod so a cattle-boat limit-load and a
  private-boat score are comparable.
- **No date = not current.** A report with no date is not current intel and does
  not get the current-layer weight; it drops to the pattern layer at best. The
  hard report cutoff still stands — stale reports feed priors, never the plan's
  current-conditions read.
