# Swimbaits

Execution for soft-plastic swimbaits and slugs on kelp and reef bass. The core
skill set: **rig it weedless so it runs clean through structure, wind it fast
enough to track, and set on the pause — not the grab.** Where the fish hold and
which structure to work is the router
([calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md),
[barred sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md)); this note owns the rigging, the
retrieve, and the boat work.

## Reach for this when

- **Kelp or reef bass** and you need a bait that comes through cover clean —
  [calico](../species/calico-bass.md) up in the stringers, [sand
  bass](../species/sand-bass.md) over structure and hard bottom.
- You are **covering water** to locate biting fish along a bed edge rather than
  picking apart one spot — read the current and structure with
  [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

## Weedless rigging (Erik Landesfeind)

- A **7-inch weedless / MC slug** on a **keel-weighted hook** — Gamakatsu
  **7/0, 3/8 oz** or a Trokar keel-weighted hook. Weights come **half oz, 3/4 oz,
  or nano**; Landesfeind prefers the heavier **3/4 oz** because it **keeps the
  bait in the water while you wind fast**.
- Replace the stock hook spring with a stronger one — an **Owner large spring**
  or **Trokar/Eagle Claw XL spring**. Bury the spring **all the way to the bait
  so the plastic butts up against the hook** — that keeps the bait tracking and
  makes it durable enough to land roughly **10 times as many fish on a single
  bait**.
- Line: heavy leader against kelp and rock. Landesfeind runs **50 to 65 lb
  braid** on a **300-size reel** with **60 lb fluorocarbon leader** — the leader
  gets braided up and chafed just from throwing it in the kelp, so keep it stout.

## The retrieve and the hookset

- **Fast, steady wind** to keep the bait tracking; speed varies with whether you
  fish **with, against, or perpendicular to the current**. On the surface-slug
  presentation you want the bait to **skitter along the top, occasionally popping
  out — but in the water more than out of it**, on a **one-two-three, pause**
  cadence. On the pause the bait **glides off to one side** like a freshwater
  glide bait.
- **Set on the pause, never on the grab.** The fish comes up from behind and eats
  it; you **watch it eat, pause to let it turn away from you, then set.** Swing
  the instant you get bit and **nine times out of ten you pull the bait out of
  the fish's mouth.**
- Use a **graphite-composite rod with a soft glass tip** (Landesfeind fishes a
  7-foot Rainshadow Judge live-bait blank; a 7- or 8-foot graphite rod also
  works, a 9-footer for bomb casts at San Clemente). The soft tip **bends and
  buys the split second** to drop the rod tip before the set — a stiff XH rod
  just pulls the bait free.

## Depth and where you throw it

- **Weedless / slug up high:** short, targeted casts to **individual kelp
  stringers**, starting at the **leading edge** of the bed. The slug draws fish
  in from a distance and pulls them off structure, so bomb casts work; the
  weedless is a **short, pinpoint cast** to the stringers you can see.
- **Lead-head swimbait deeper:** sink the same class of bait **70 to 80 feet down
  for calicos** over structure — fish holding up and feeding will rise to meet it.

## Boat work — covering water (Erik Landesfeind)

- **Whoever is at the wheel positions the boat** to keep the caster on the
  productive edge; the natural drift is usually in toward the beach, so swing the
  bow out to feed the angler the **outside leading-edge stringers**.
- **Cover water:** fish a bed **20 to 25 casts** if you're getting bites, only
  **10 or 12 casts** if you're not, then move. Locating biting fish is a numbers
  game across many beds; slow down and pick apart only where you get bites or see
  followers.
- Color matters little — a **green-top / gold-bottom** ("hot koch") is a
  confidence pattern that works cloudy or sunny, warm or cold. Fish what you
  believe in.

## Double swimbait rig (Nick, California Current)

Run **two swimbaits off a three-way swivel** to pick up the **followers** that
trail a hooked bass — the second bait dangling alongside the fought fish gets
eaten and lifts the catch rate.

- **Main line → three-way swivel** (San Diego jam knot). Off one arm, a **shorter
  leader (~12 in)** to the **smaller bait up ahead** (e.g. a 5-inch); off the
  other, a **longer leader (~24 in)** to the **bigger main lure** (e.g. a 7-inch)
  running behind. The **12 in / 24 in** split keeps the two baits separated.
- Nick runs **40 lb Seaguar Blue Label** leader on a Tranx 300 with **65 lb
  PowerPro** and an 8-ft heavy rod — heavier gear, fished **up high in the kelp**
  in warm summer water when the fish are chasing bait.

## Common failures

- **Swinging on the grab** instead of pausing — the single biggest cause of
  pulled baits, on both weedless and slug.
- **Too stiff a rod (XH)** — no tip flex, pulls baits out of mouths; want the rod
  to bend a bit once hooked.
- **Leader too light in kelp/rock** — it chafes and braids up; run it heavy.
- **Loose spring / bait not butted to the hook** — the bait slides down, stops
  tracking, and tears off; bury the spring to the plastic.
- **Camping a dead bed** — if the bites aren't there in a dozen casts, move.
