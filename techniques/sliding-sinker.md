# Sliding Sinker

Execution for the sliding-sinker (Carolina) rig: the **weight rides free on the
main line above a swivel, and a leader runs from the swivel to the hook**, so a
fish can pick up the bait and swim off **without feeling the lead**. The line
slides through the sinker — the resistance the fish would otherwise feel is
gone, which is what makes it deadly on wary, soft-bottom feeders. Where these
fish sit is the router; this note owns the rig and how you feed the bite.

## Reach for this when

- **Sand or soft bottom** where the bait should waft naturally just off the
  deck — [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) is the headline
  target; also [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) and
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) on the drift or the anchor.
- The fish are **wary** and drop a bait the instant they feel weight — the
  free-sliding lead removes that tell.
- You are **drifting bait** and want the presentation pinned near bottom while
  it still moves with the current.

## Building the rig

- **Egg sinker → bead → barrel swivel → leader → hook.** The bead protects the
  knot from the sliding sinker and the swivel stops the weight; the leader length
  sets how far the bait rides behind and above the lead — run it long enough that
  the bait moves freely, short enough to stay in the strike zone.
- Match the sinker to the drift and the depth — just heavy enough to tick bottom.
  A fish that grabs the bait **pulls line through the sinker and feels nothing**;
  that is the whole point of the rig.

## The squid version (Duane Diego Malloy)

- For Channel Islands squid fishing Malloy runs a **small sliding egg sinker
  straight to a Gamakatsu offset octopus hook**, sinker **preferably glow color**
  — it matches the squid and adds a light attractant on the common **night**
  bite.
- Rig the squid through the top of the mantle, **slide it up onto the barb and
  shank, pop the point out the back, then slide the mantle over the eye and knot**
  to lock it in place. **Fish two** squid — it mimics the spawning ball the fish
  are there to feed on.
- Result on the water: the **egg sinker bounces off the bottom and the bait
  wafts behind it** in the current, which triggers bites. Strong on both the
  anchor and the drift.

## Fishing it — feed the bite

- Let the sinker settle to the bottom and keep it in light contact. When a fish
  loads up, **let it take** — because the line runs through the lead, you can
  give slack and let the fish turn before you come tight, rather than pulling
  against a weight it can feel.

## Gear class

Drift / bait-on-the-bottom class — see
[gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md). Octopus offset hooks; often run
alongside soaking [dropper-loop](dropper-loop.md) rods.

## Common failures

- **Leader too short** — the bait sits on top of the weight instead of wafting,
  and the fish feels the lead.
- **Too much weight** — drags out of the zone in current and telegraphs
  resistance; use the least lead that holds bottom.
- **Coming tight too soon** — the rig's advantage is the free take; don't rush
  the hookset and undo it.
