# Flyline

Execution for flylining live bait: a hooked live bait fished **with no weight**,
left to swim on its own out and away from the boat. The most natural presentation
in the box — the whole game is a lively bait, the right hook, and the right hook
position for the situation. Bait keeping and selection depth live in
[fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).

## Reach for this when

- Live **bait is in the water** and gamefish are on the chum or the bait line —
  [tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md),
  [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md),
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md),
  [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md), or
  [dorado](../species/dorado.md) working a paddy or the boat.

## Hook position by situation

Pick the hook position for the swimming behavior you want:

- **Nose hook** — through the clear spot right in front of the eyes. The
  all-purpose flyline hook: the bait swims naturally, stays up, and can also be
  slow-trolled. Most reactive, most lively.
- **Shoulder / dorsal hook** (mackerel especially) — keeps your **line up out of
  the water** and holds the bait **high, skating across the top** for a reaction
  bite.
- **Belly hook** — just above the anal fin. Sends the bait **down and away** from
  the boat, deeper in the column.
- **Butt hook** — near the vent. Also **down and away**, and it defeats **sea
  lions** at the islands that pick off nose-hooked baits, while still flylining
  and swimming well.

## Bait and hook selection

- **Match the hook to the bait, not to the fish.** A bait dragging an oversized
  hook won't swim right and won't get bit; size the hook to the sardine, mackerel
  or anchovy in your hand — a 1/0 live-bait hook is a common all-around size,
  step up to 2/0–3/0 on big donkey baits, step down on small bait. See
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **Bait choice:** sardine (the SoCal staple), **mackerel** (heartier, sometimes
  preferred), or anchovy — nose-hook location is the same clear spot on all
  three.
- **Bait selection is king.** Cull the bloody, scaly, lethargic baits into the
  chum bucket; fish the lively lime-green ones that are hardest to catch in the
  tank. If a bait isn't pulling line off the reel within about **60 seconds**,
  wind it in, flick it off, and grab a fresh one.
- **Circle hooks:** hold the hook **high on the arch and press straight down**
  into the bait — holding it from the bottom leaves no gap and mangles the bait.

## Fishing it

- **No weight** — let the bait swim. **Cast well away from the boat**; fish are
  finickier close to the hull, and a bait sent out and away gets bit far more
  often.
- On butt- or belly-hooked baits, **keep tension** the moment it lands: a little
  pull makes the bait swim away from you like a propeller. Give it slack and it
  turns and swims right back under the boat.

## Gear class

- A **soft / softer rod class** — a moderate, forgiving tip loads on the run and
  keeps a light bait swimming naturally, and cushions the strike on light leader.
  See [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Common failures

- **Hook too big for the bait** — it can't swim, so it doesn't get bit.
- Leaving a **dead or lethargic bait** out past the 60-second window.
- **Slack** on a butt/belly bait, letting it swim back under the boat.
- Wrong hook position for the conditions — nose-hooking into a pack of **sea
  lions** that then eat every bait.
