# Slow-Pitch Jigging (SPJ)

Work an asymmetric metal jig so it **flutters on the fall** like a wounded,
dying baitfish — the fall is the trigger, not the retrieve. A soft, parabolic rod
flicks the jig up and lets it shimmer down; strikes come from below or the side.
Execution only; species and situations are the reverse map in **Linked from**.

## Reach for this when

- Bottom [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) on high spots (the BOLA
  September/spring program), plus school-grade tuna and any deep gamefish that
  will eat a fluttering jig.
- [Rockfish and lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) when you want to turn
  bottom fishing into sport fishing — bigger average fish on light gear.
- Contrast with [knife/speed jigging](knife-jigging.md): SPJ works the fall;
  speed jigging rips a slender jig up through the column to imitate a *fleeing*
  bait. Different jigs, different strikes.

## Mechanics — the pitch and the fall

- Grip: **palm the reel**, index finger under the spool and thumb on top, with
  the exposed rear blank tucked **under your forearm** as the fulcrum (not under
  the armpit like speed jigging) for a wide range of motion.
- Load the rod off that fulcrum and let it unload — the tip **flicks the jig up**,
  then you feed slack and **watch/feel the fall.** Most bites come as a bump on
  the drop; wind tight into them, don't swing.
- **Match the jig to the rod's rating.** SPJ rods are rated by max jig weight; the
  right weight is what makes the blank load and impart the action. A **350 g-max**
  rod is the most useful all-rounder to start; a lighter 250 g-rated rod is the
  most fun for bottom fish, a 450 g-rated rod for bigger pelagics.
- Fight off the reel: point the rod down toward the fish and **winch** — keep the
  blank roughly parallel to the water, never lift-and-pump past ~45°, or you
  overload the soft tip. The rod gets the bite; the reel fights the fish.

## Jig weight & selection

- **Weight rule: ~1.5 g per foot of depth** for slow-pitch (vs ~1 g/ft for a
  [speed jig](knife-jigging.md)) — the extra weight offsets the flutter and keeps
  the jig vertical.
- **High current or wind → longer, more slender jig** (cuts water, falls fast and
  straight); **slack / light wind → fatter, fluttery jig** (more hang time and
  action on the fall). Vertical "knife-shaped" SPJ jigs punch down to a deep zone
  and stay there in a fast drift; wing-fall / butterfly jigs give the traditional
  wide rocking flutter.
- BOLA yellowtail zone reference: **150–300 ft on high spots**, fishing observed
  at **150–180 ft**, **130–250 g wing-fall** jigs eating well.

## Gear-class detail

- **Rod:** dedicated SPJ blank — thin, very high carbon, extremely parabolic tip,
  ~6.5–7 ft, exposed rear grip. See [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Line — thin braid is the whole point.** No stretch for action, and thin
  diameter so the jig **falls straight down**; even a jump from ~20 lb to 30/40/50
  lb braid adds scope and blows the jig sideways. Deeper water → thinner braid.
  A proven rockfish rig: **20 lb braid** main → **25 ft of 50 lb braid** (abrasion
  over pinnacles) → four-turn surgeon's knot to **~3 ft of 80 lb** mono or fluoro
  at the jig. For pelagics, step up to a two-speed reel and ~**40 lb braid**.
  Connect braid-to-leader with a thin knot (PR/FG class) so it clears the small
  guides.
- **Jig:** [knife/slow-pitch jig class](../lures/knife-jigs.md). US waters cap you
  at **two hooks** — single assist on the nose, single on the tail (an assist
  *pair* on the front already puts you over the limit). A **6 in Gulp curly-tail
  trailer** is a strong scent activator in productive zones (grub tails on the
  tail hook; see the [dropper-loop](dropper-loop.md) note's Gulp handling).

## Common failures

- **Braid too heavy** — the jig scopes away and never falls straight; the fall
  action dies, and so does the bite.
- **Jig weight mismatched to depth/current** — too light and it blows sideways in
  current; too heavy and the flutter is gone. Start at ~1.5 g/ft and adjust.
- **Fighting with the rod** — lifting past 45° overloads the tip and breaks rods.
  Winch off the reel.
