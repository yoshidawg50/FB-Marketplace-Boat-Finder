# Chunking

Execution for chunking: cutting bait into pieces to build a **chum line**, then
drifting a hook bait back through it so it looks like just another falling chunk.
The tool for fish you can see or mark but can't get to bite. Where the fish are
and why they've shut off are the routers:
[dorado](../species/dorado.md) and [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).

## Reach for this when

- Fish are **marking on the meter or visible but not biting** — the chunk line is
  what wakes them up.
- **Dorado on a paddy** that has gone quiet — chunking gets a shut-off school
  competing again. See [dorado](../species/dorado.md).
- **Tuna at anchor or on a drift** where a live-bait flyline alone isn't drawing
  them up. See [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).

## Building the chum line

- Cut bait into pieces and feed them **steadily, a few at a time** — enough to
  hold a scent and sink trail without stuffing the fish. The chunk line is an
  artificial current-fed feed lane leading back to your hook bait.
- Watch the **drift angle of the chunks** relative to the boat — it reads the
  true current and tells you where your bait needs to be. If the chunks and the
  boat lie the same way, there's no current working the line.

## Drifting the hook bait

- **Match the hook bait's fall to the chunks.** Free-spool line so the hooked
  cut bait sinks back **at the same rate** as the free chunks around it — any
  drag that makes it fall unnaturally or hang in the column gives it away.
- Feed line as the drift takes it; a chunk that behaves exactly like the others
  is the one that gets eaten.

## On a paddy (dorado wake-up)

- A quiet paddy holding dorado often just needs a trigger: chunking cut bait
  around the kelp gets the school fired up and racing for food, at which point a
  drifted bait or a hooked chunk gets crushed. Chunking sits alongside the other
  paddy wake-up tricks in the [dorado](../species/dorado.md) note.

## Common failures

- **Over-chumming** — feeding so much cut bait the fish fill up on free chunks
  and ignore the hook.
- **Under-reading the current** — dropping chunks with no current to carry them,
  so there's no line for fish to follow.
- A hook bait that **falls wrong** — too much line drag, so it doesn't match the
  free chunks and the fish refuse it.
