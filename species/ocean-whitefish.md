# Ocean Whitefish

The **ocean whitefish** (*Caulolatilus princeps*) is a **tilefish**, not a
rockfish — a distinct, highly underrated table fish that schools over hard bottom
and sand edges and eats the same bottom presentations as the reds. At the
**Channel Islands** it is a **prime variety target**: "they school up like crazy
up here — you'll catch a hundred of them," and they run big enough that a good
one is a legit 30-lb-class dropper-loop fish (6-8KfjEg0x8). It gets its own router
because it fishes and routes differently from the rockfish stack even when they
share a spot (see Doctrine & conflicts).

## Where & when

- **Where:** **Channel Islands hard bottom and sand-edge transitions** — Santa
  Cruz, Santa Rosa, Santa Barbara Island and the outer island reefs. They hold
  over **hard bottom and along the hard-to-sand seams**, often the same shallow
  island high spots (100–250 ft) that produce the rockfish/lingcod variety bag.
  See [island structure](../locations/island-structure.md), [zone lexicon](../locations/zone-lexicon.md).
- **When:** an all-season island bottomfish; best fished on a **variety day**
  when you're already parked on a good island rock. They school, so once you find
  one you're usually into a pile.
- **Conditions:** like everything at the islands, **current is as important as
  the bait** — a slack spot meters fish that won't eat (6-8KfjEg0x8). See
  [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

No surface sign — a **meter-and-chart** fishery like the rockfish complex.

- **Sonar:** hunt **hard bottom and the hard-to-sand transition** on the sounder.
  Whitefish paint as **scattered mid-column marks and arches over hard bottom and
  along the sand edge** — often the "scratchy marks through the column" you see
  while you're set up on an island rock, higher off the bottom than the rockfish
  stack sitting tight to the pinnacle. Run a **dual-frequency split screen** (low
  to find the structure and bait, high to pick the gamefish out). Mark the
  hard-bottom seams as waypoints and work them. Full method:
  [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
  [search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
- **Read:** a spot that lights up with **stacked, willing mid-column marks that
  bite immediately on a dropped squid** is usually whitefish schooling — they are
  a fast, aggressive eat compared with picking a lingcod off the bottom.

## Situations → techniques

| Situation (conditions) | Do this | Gear class | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parked on island hard bottom, squid in the tank | **[Dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md)** with squid on the bottom | 30 lb dropper-loop / bottom-gangion class, level-wind reel | The bread-and-butter — a legit 30-lb-class rig; they eat squid readily and school, so keep dropping |
| Scratchy marks through the whole column | **[Sliding-sinker rig](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)** with squid | 30 lb bait class | Covers **every level of the column** from surface to bottom — the go-to at the islands when the marks aren't pinned to the bottom (6-8KfjEg0x8) |
| Want to cover water / no bait aboard | **Lead-head slug or small jig** on the bottom | 3 oz lead-head / light iron class | Same bottom presentation as the reds; whitefish will chase a slug or [gulp grub](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md) |

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Rod/reel:** the same **island bottom setup** as the rockfish complex — a
  **30 lb-class dropper-loop rig on a level-wind reel** handles them. See
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Terminal:** **[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md)** or
  **[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)**; squid is the staple bait,
  cut bait and **[gulp/soft-plastic grubs](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)**
  work when you're not carrying squid.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- **Separated from [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) by
  design (build decision).** Ocean whitefish is a **tilefish (Malacanthidae)**,
  not a *Sebastes* rockfish — different fish, and it holds **higher off the bottom
  and along sand edges** rather than pinned to the rock. Keeping it as its own
  router means a variety-day plan can call it out explicitly (it is often *the*
  reason to stop on a mediocre-looking sand-edge seam) instead of burying it
  inside the rockfish note. They co-occur on island spots and share terminal
  tackle, so the two notes cross-link rather than merge.
- **Confidence: medium** — the standout SoCal-specific source is the single
  Channel Islands variety day; the value ("underrated," "catch a hundred," 30-lb
  class) is a single credible on-water account.
