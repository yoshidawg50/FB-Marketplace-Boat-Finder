# Hollow-Core Splice & Serving

Two knotless connections made possible by hollow-core braid, both relying on the
same finger-trap principle: braid woven around a buried line clamps tighter as
load pulls the join. **Hollow-to-hollow splicing** joins two hollow braids by
threading each inside the other — the seamless way to add a fresh top shot after
you lose line, without stripping and re-spooling. **Serving a fluoro leader into
hollow core** buries a fluorocarbon leader straight into the braid and secures it
with a thread wrap — a rod that's rigged this way needs no leader knot at all.
Both build on the [line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md) system and are
the foundation under a built [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md).

## When to use it

- **Hollow-to-hollow splice** — you break off mid-fight and lose ~150 yards; add
  a new top shot to hollow-core braid so the join disappears into the line and
  saves you re-spooling an expensive full reel. This is prep-bench work, not
  something to attempt on a pitching deck.
- **Served fluoro leader** — stealth bait fishing where a leader knot would show,
  e.g. Guadalupe Island; dedicate rods (a 50 lb, a 60 lb, an 80 lb) each with its
  fluoro already served in and ready to fish, so there's no on-the-water retie.
- Both require **hollow-core** braid; neither works with solid braid.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Splice overlap:** bury about **18 in of each line into the other** — one line
  served into the second, and the second served back into the first, so the join
  is doubled through the middle. Push the braids together to open the weave, then
  feed the needle straight; a kinked needle or a bent line makes it fight you.
- **Splice finish:** none needed — pull excess through, trim it, and it hides
  back inside. The only sign left is a slight thickness where the two lines
  overlap, dropping back to single-strand braid at each end.
- **Serving bury length:** serve the fluoro **a minimum of 3 ft, preferably 4 ft**
  into the hollow braid. Size the hollow rigging needle to the leader (e.g. a
  needle sized for 60 lb fluoro); seat the fluoro tip in the needle and tack it
  with a lighter so it feeds without slipping off.
- **Serving thread & wrap:** after burying the leader, clamp the junction and
  lock it with a serving thread from a spindle (a **6–8 lb solid braid** works).
  Start the wrap ~3 in up with a constrictor knot (some tie a plain half hitch)
  pulled tight, follow with a half hitch, then wrap down over the braid-and-fluoro
  junction. The wraps tighten as they go. Finish with a few half hitches and a
  final three-wrap hitch, cinch, trim close, and set with a dab of super glue.
  The wrap is a security lock — the finger-trap already carries the load.
- **Failure modes:** too short a bury (the finger-trap can't grip); pushing the
  needle through the braid wall instead of down the core; skipping the serve so
  the leader creeps out under slack; kinking the needle so the line won't feed.
