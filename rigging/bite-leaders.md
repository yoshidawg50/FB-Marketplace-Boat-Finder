# Bluefin Bite Leaders

A bite leader is a short, heavy fluorocarbon section crimped between the main
line and the jig — described as the most critical part of a
[bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) knife-jig setup. A hooked tuna often
inhales the jig deep, and you don't want braid or lighter mono sawing against its
teeth and gill rakers where the fish can cut you off. The heavy fluoro takes that
abuse. Because you're geared for the biggest fish that could eat — anything from a
20 lb schoolie to a 300 lb trophy on the same drop — the bite leader is sized for
the worst case. It seats on the [line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md)
system between the braid main line and the lure.

## When to use it

- Standard on [knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md) for bluefin, where
  a 200–300 g jig drops straight up and down and any grade of fish may hit.
  Especially the night bite, when the bigger fish tend to come and anglers run
  the heaviest gear.
- Complements the trolling and casting approaches in
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and
  [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-trolling.md) — the bite leader is the
  jig-fishing terminal, not a troll rig.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Length:** keep it short — **no more than about 2 to 2.5 ft**. It carries a
  large swivel at the top that will **not** wind through the guides, so a leader
  that's too long strands you at your top-shot connection with the fish still at
  deep color and no line left to wind.
- **Fluoro class:** heavy, abrasion-resistant fluoro — the example builds on
  **130 lb** leader. Main line is typically **80–100 lb braid**, sometimes with a
  short shock leader above.
- **Match crimp to leader:** on 130 lb fluoro, use a **size 1.6 crimp** and a
  matching **1.6 chafe sleeve**. The sleeve protects the little loop of mono that
  rides around the lure's ring. Crimpers are numbered to the sleeve size — seat
  the crimp in the matching slot (e.g. the 1.5–2 slot).
- **Crimp judgment (mono/fluoro):** do **not** over-crimp — snug is enough, two
  presses per crimp (one top, one bottom). Over-crimping bites into and weakens
  the fluoro. Leave a little tag through the second side of the crimp so the loop
  can't pull back.
- **Connection to the main line:** crimp a large swivel to the top end (that
  swivel is why it can't pass the guides).
- **Connection to the jig:** for a dedicated single-jig leader, crimp **directly
  through the lure's solid wire ring, not through a split ring** — fewer twists
  and the strongest join. Use a split ring only if you want one bite leader you
  swap across several jigs.
- **Failure modes:** leader too long to wind (swivel jams at the tip); crimp
  mismatched to leader diameter; over-crimped fluoro; too little tag through the
  crimp; connecting through a weak split ring on a big-fish jig.
