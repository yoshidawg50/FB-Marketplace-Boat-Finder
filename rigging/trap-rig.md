# Trap Rig

A two-hook live-bait rig for halibut: a J hook up front with an additional
stinger hook trailing behind. The stinger covers the back half of the bait, so
the short, tail-grabbing bites halibut are known for still find a hook.

## When to use it

- Drifting live bait for [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md),
  described here for shallow-water halibut off Catalina.
- A refinement of the basic [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)
  live-bait drift, adding a stinger to convert more short bites into hookups.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Components:** a lead J hook with an additional stinger hook — a treble — set
  behind it.
- **Bait rigging (sardine):** nose-hook the bait with the J hook right in front
  of the eyes, then place the treble stinger into the back half of the fish with
  a little bit of slack left in the connection between the two hooks.
- **Why the stinger:** halibut commonly grab and hold the tail end of a bait; the
  trailing treble on the back half is what capitalizes on those bites that a
  single nose hook would miss.
- **Judgment:** keep a little slack to the stinger so the bait swims naturally
  rather than being bound tight between the two hooks.
