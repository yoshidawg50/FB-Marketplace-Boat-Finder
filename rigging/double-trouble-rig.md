# Double Trouble Rig

A two-bait live-bait presentation hung from the kite and run off two rods —
similar in the water to flying a kite bait, but with two live baits instead of a
flyer. Two crimped hook droppers share one swivel so both baits ride together
right at the surface.

## When to use it

- A live-bait alternative to the flying fish off the kite for
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md), fished when conditions are right.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Line class by grade of fish:** for fish around 20 to 40 pounders, a lighter
  100-pound line; for bigger fish, 150-pound or 200-pound line. The demo is built
  on 150-pound test (Seaguar Big Game fluorocarbon).
- **Hook size by line class:** 100- and 150-pound class line pairs with size 4/0
  hooks (varies by brand); stepping up to 200-pound test line calls for a bigger
  hook. The demo uses 4/0 circle hooks.
- **Build:** a little more than an arm's length of leader, crimped throughout
  (crimp sizes matched to line diameter). One crimped loop holds a heavy-duty
  swivel — a highly rated swivel, on the order of a 200/300 rating — with a small
  tag-end loop left so the swivel has movement. The two hook droppers are crimped
  on, each with a burned tag end flattened to act as a stopper against its crimp.
- **The critical adjustment:** the two hooks must be brought as parallel and even
  as possible so the baits are not offset. If one dropper hangs a few inches
  higher, one bait fishes at the surface and the other does not — the goal is both
  baits together right at the surface.
- **Hooking the baits:** match the bait to the grade of fish. Do not nose-hook
  as you would a flyline bait; collar-hook or shoulder-hook instead, so the baits
  plane out flat as they go out rather than tilting nose-up.
