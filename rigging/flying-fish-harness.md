# Flying Fish Harness

The pre-rigged flying fish (dead natural, netted or sabiki-caught, or an
artificial) used off the kite for large local Pacific bluefin. The rig combines
a body harness — skewers and zip ties that lock the wings open and set a
lifelike, tail-down profile — with a two-hook leader so the presentation swims
naturally across the surface and holds up through repeated deployments.

## When to use it

- [Kite fishing](../techniques/kite-fishing.md) offshore for
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md), described here for the 100- to
  300-pound grade of fish.
- The flying fish is one of the most preferred kite baits for that class; the
  average angler can catch flyers on a sabiki, but it is tough, so most buy them
  at the local tackle shop.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Wing harness:** a skewer is set in behind the shoulder just above the spine;
  a second skewer opens a hole between the ribs and the wing where you want the
  wing positioned. The wings are held open with zip ties through additional
  holes, done to both sides. Everything must be cinched tight so there is no
  slippage along the skewers during deployment.
- **Body/profile skewer:** a skewer run down the middle of the limp fish, entered
  through the mouth. Push it about three quarters of the way in, then lift the
  tail as you finish so the bait sits with a slight down-and-tail-in-the-water
  profile that lets it "do its thing" at the surface.
- **Mouth skewer:** a final skewer pins the mouth closed. This keeps the bait
  presenting longer and stops water from being scooped through the lower lip and
  out the roof of the mouth.
- **Hook rig:** a standard San Diego bluefin flyer rig — a large J hook with a
  large treble hook trailing off the J as a stinger. Leader is 200 to 400 pound
  mono, typically 400. The main leader runs to the kite rod; the treble stinger
  is set into the back of the fish, inserted just behind the body stick under the
  spine and out the other side, then positioned and secured.
- **Failure modes:** loose skewers/zip ties let the harness slip and kill the
  action; too light a leader risks a bite-off on this grade of fish. Rig 10 to
  12 flyers for a four-angler day and store them neatly in the cooler so they are
  ready to deploy.
