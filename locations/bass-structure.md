# Bass Structure

How to break down the kelp, reef, and hard-bottom structure that
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) and
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md) live on — reading it and setting up on it.
Universal structure knowledge; the species notes hold the behavior and technique
routing. Sources: Academy Ep. 5 (bass structure) and Ep. 14 (kelp calico),
Landesfeind.

## The structure types

- **Kelp.** The stringers, the outer edge, the pockets and lanes inside the bed,
  and the deep-water side where the kelp grows off harder bottom. Calico relate to
  the kelp; the **edges and pinch-points** produce, not the dense middle.
- **Reef / hard bottom.** Rock, boiler rocks, high spots, and the **hard-to-soft
  transition** where reef meets sand. Sand bass hold on and around hard bottom and
  the deeper structure; calico work the reef edges too.
- **The edge is the spot.** Across all of it, the productive water is a **defined
  edge** — kelp-to-open, reef-to-sand, high-spot-to-flat — where current pins bait
  against structure.

## Current is the switch

Bass structure only fishes when the current sets up an edge on it. The
**parallel-current rule** governs (Ep. 14): current running **parallel** to the
structure creates a definable leading edge and makes reef pinch-points work;
**into** the structure is marginal; **off** it is dead. The full rule, the
typology (which structures are two-directional), and the late-fall into-the-island
exception are in [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

**Read the current off the kelp itself** (Ep. 14): kelp **pulled 20 ft under** =
raging current (surface presentations dead); kelp **laid down** = strong; kelp
**resurfacing** = slacking, switch your presentation back. These on-water tells
are collected in [current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md).

## Reading bait on the meter

Structure with the wrong bait picture is not worth stopping on. A **solid,
unbroken wall of bait** on the meter usually has **no predators** on it; **broken,
harried, or moving bait** does (Ep. 5). Grade the mark before you commit — the
same principle that governs stopping on offshore marks; see
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

## Setting up

Work the **up-current edge** so your bait or jig sweeps down onto the structure
the way current-borne food does. On anchor, standoff scales to the fish: bottom
fish → on the spot; midwater/gamefish → up-current, standoff scaled to current
strength; break-off-prone yellows → further still (see the anchoring geometry in
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)). The general
current-over-structure mechanism ties it together in
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).
