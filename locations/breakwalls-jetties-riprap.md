# Break Walls, Jetties and Riprap

Man-made rock structure — harbor break walls, jetties, and riprap channel
edges — read as fishing structure. Universal knowledge; the species behavior
lives in the species notes ([calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md),
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md),
[spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md)). Sources: Academy Ep. 9
(understanding break walls / jetties / riprap) and Ep. 10 (tackle & techniques
for them), Landesfeind.

## Riprap is a current structure

A break wall or jetty looks like a uniform line of rock, but it does not fish
uniformly. What makes riprap produce is the **deviations** in the rock line —
points, pockets, corners, notches, and where the wall changes angle. These are
**"thumb over the hose"** features: current forced past a deviation accelerates
and upwells there, exactly the way the current-over-structure chain works on
offshore banks (see [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
Fish hold on and behind the deviation, on the down-current side of the current
break, waiting for the accelerated water to bring food past.

The read, then, is: **skip the featureless straightaways; fish the corners,
points, pockets, and angle changes.** A break wall's ends and its inside/outside
corners are prime because the deviation is largest there.

## Birds mark the deviation

You do not have to guess where the deviations are: **birds sitting on the rocks
mark the thumb-over-hose deviation** (Ep. 9). A cluster of birds parked on one
stretch of a wall is sitting where the current break concentrates bait — that is
your spot on the rock line. This is the inshore riprap version of the broader
rule that **birds on structure mark a repeatable decision**; the bird doctrine is
in [bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md).

## Setting up on it

Approach from the **up-current side** and work your presentation so it sweeps
down onto the deviation with the current, the way food arrives. As with all rock
structure, position so a hooked fish can be pulled **away** from the rocks rather
than into them. Ep. 10 covers the tackle and techniques (jig and bait
presentations worked tight to the rock); pair this structure read with the
species notes above for the retrieve and gear. The same rock-deviation logic
applies in miniature to the riprap edges inside
[bays and harbors](bays-and-harbors.md).
