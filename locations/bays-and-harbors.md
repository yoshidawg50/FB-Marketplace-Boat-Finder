# Bays and Harbors

How a SoCal bay or harbor is laid out for fishing — the universal structure a
new-to-SoCal angler should be able to name and read on any of them (San Diego
Bay, Mission Bay, Newport, Huntington Harbour, the LA/Long Beach complex). This
note is the *structure*; how each species uses it lives in the species notes —
[spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) and
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) are the two you will fish
these features for most. Everything here is from Academy Ep. 2 (Landesfeind,
bays & harbors).

## The tidal engine

A bay is a **tidal system first**. Water pours in and drains out through the
mouth and the channels on every tide, and that flow is what turns the structure
on. Tide amplitude couples to the moon — **springs on the full and new, neaps on
the quarters** — so the same bay moves far more water on a spring tide than a
neap. Slack and the moving-water windows drive the bite; see
[tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md). Note the extreme: harbor
spotted bay bass are **fully tide-driven**, unlike the current-driven,
tide-indifferent calico on the outside.

## The structure inventory

Read a bay by finding these features and the **edges** where two of them meet:

- **Eelgrass flats.** The bay's primary cover and forage habitat — grass beds on
  the shallow flats hold bait, shrimp, and crabs. The **edge of the grass** (grass
  meeting sand or a channel drop) is the ambush line, not the middle of the bed.
- **Channels and channel edges.** The deep lanes that carry the tidal flow. The
  **drop-off from flat to channel** is a current-swept edge that concentrates
  fish on a moving tide — fish sit on the down-current side and let the tide
  bring food to them.
- **Docks and pilings.** Vertical structure and shade. Dock lines, piling fields,
  and the shaded water under floats give fish overhead cover and current breaks.
- **Riprap edges.** The rock lining channels, jetty bases, and harbor mouths.
  These behave like the outside riprap in miniature — see
  [break walls, jetties and riprap](breakwalls-jetties-riprap.md) for how the
  rock deviations ("thumb over the hose") concentrate current and fish, and how
  birds on the rocks mark them.

## Reading the flow through the bay

The whole system is directional: on the **flood**, water and bait push **in**
through the mouth and up the channels; on the **ebb**, everything drains back out.
Set up **up-current of the structure** so your bait sweeps naturally down onto it,
and re-read the picture when the tide turns — a spot that was on the up-current
side of a channel edge on the flood is on the wrong side after the switch. The
current-over-structure logic is the same one that governs the outside coast; the
mechanism is in
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).
