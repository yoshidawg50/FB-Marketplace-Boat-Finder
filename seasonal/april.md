# April

**These are priors — the pattern layer, not current intel.** They tell you what
the Bight usually does in April so you can weight zones before pulling live
[BightSST](../planning/day-plan-protocol.md) conditions. Do not treat a stale
report as today's water.

## Bluefin — far south, and mostly out of skiff range

- **[Bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) are still far south** in April, ahead of the
  volume that arrives later. It is a **sonar-school + night-jig fishery** — mark
  the schools deep on the meter, fish them at night — and mostly **out of skiff
  range**.
- **The sportboat advantage is being in the zone before gray light.** Range and
  overnight running let a sportboat set up on the schools before first light;
  a small boat usually cannot get there in time. Plan April bluefin as a
  long-range or overnight proposition, not a run-and-gun day.

## Wind-rollover geography — who blows out and who holds

A big NW blow does **not** hit the whole Bight equally. Know which water rolls
over so a bad forecast doesn't blank your whole plan (4/21/22):

- **The northern Channel Islands and the LA/Ventura coastal shelf crash
  5–8 °F** in a big NW blow — wind-wave-driven rollover, cold and off.
- **South of Newport is "less prone to wind-wave-driven rollovers."** The
  geography shelters it.
- **The Coronados, the tuna grounds, Catalina, and San Clemente Island held
  through it.** When the north blows out, these are the zones that stay
  fishable.

See [sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md) and
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md) for the mechanism, and
[bight geography](../locations/bight-geography.md) for the zones.

## Coronado yellowtail — "seeing-not-biting"

**[Coronado yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) run "seeing-not-biting" cycles with
narrow daily windows.** In April you will often *see* fish — meter them, spot
them — without a bite, and the window that does open is short. Be on the fish
and ready when it comes rather than expecting an all-day grind; see
[tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md) for timing the window.

## San Clemente Island squid zone — depth, not departure

**When the squid moves deep, the SCI squid-zone bite dies — but the fish did
not leave.** This is a **relocation** problem: the forage dropped out of the
zone you were fishing, so follow it down or move to where it stacked. Read the
squid on the meter before you write the spot off.
