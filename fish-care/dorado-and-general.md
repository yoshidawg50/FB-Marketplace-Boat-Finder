# Dorado & General Fish Care

[Dorado](../species/dorado.md) get cared for differently from tuna — the priority
is protecting their color and flesh, not bleeding — and the second half of this
note collects the icing and slurry principles that hold across every species.
Care mentions are drawn from the dorado tactics video:
[dorado tactics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E4vKwRaRueA); paraphrased, not
transcribed.

## Dorado specifics

- **No bleed.** Unlike tuna, dorado are not bled. Get them cold instead.
- **Ice immediately.** Put the fish on ice as soon as it is aboard — dorado
  quality drops fast if it sits warm.
- **Never freshwater-rinse.** Do not rinse a dorado in fresh water: it dulls and
  washes out the color. Keep fresh water off the fish.
- **Bring a car cooler for the drive home.** Plan the cold chain past the dock —
  a separate cooler in the vehicle keeps the fish cold on the road home rather
  than letting it warm up on the drive.

## General principles (all species)

These apply whether or not the species gets bled:

- **The salted slurry.** A below-freezing slurry chills faster and colder than
  bare ice water: **rock salt (~2 cups per 7–10 lb bag of ice)** plus a
  **splash of seawater** to wet it. The salt is what drops the mix below
  freezing. (Slurry numbers from BD Outdoors care/seminar reporting.) The tuna
  chain uses the same slurry — see [tuna care](../fish-care/tuna-care.md).
- **Keep fish cold and out of the sun.** Get the fish into the cold quickly and
  keep it there; a fish baking on a hot deck is losing quality the whole time.
- **Don't drown fish in fresh water.** Do not soak or rinse the catch in fresh
  water — it degrades color and flesh. Keep the fish in cold salt slurry or on
  ice, not swimming in freshwater melt.
