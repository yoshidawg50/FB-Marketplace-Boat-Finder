# Ikejime (Iki-Jime)

Ikejime is spiking the fish's brain to kill it instantly, the top tier of tuna
care when quality is the goal. It sits on top of — not in place of — bleeding and
chilling. The reference here is a short walkthrough on a bluefin:
[ikejime on a bluefin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WzT0RSHpaQc). Watch it for
the exact spike location and motion; the notes below are the concept and the
judgment, paraphrased.

## What it is

- **Brain spike.** A spike is driven into the fish's **brain** to kill it
  immediately, at a target point on the head. The video shows where that point
  sits on a bluefin — the spot is what you learn by watching, not by reading.
- **Pithing.** The related step is running a wire down the spinal cord to
  destroy the nerve line (pithing). The concept is to shut the whole nervous
  system down cleanly so the fish doesn't thrash and burn quality into the meat.

## When it's worth it

- Reach for ikejime when you are after **top quality / sashimi**-grade flesh.
  An instant, clean kill leaves better meat than a fish that fights out in the
  hold.
- For a fish going to the smoker or the grill, the full ritual matters less —
  bleed and chill will carry it. Ikejime is the extra step for the best table
  fish.

## How it relates to bleeding and chilling

- Ikejime does **not** replace [bleeding and the slurry chill](../fish-care/tuna-care.md).
  The full quality chain is: kill clean (ikejime), **bleed**, field-dress, and
  **chill** in the below-freezing slurry. Each step protects the meat in a
  different way, so they stack.

## Honest scope note

The linked video is short: it demonstrates the spike on a
[bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and little more. Treat this note as
the concept and the decision of *when* to bother — the precise spike placement
and depth come from the video and from hands-on practice, not from prose here.
