# Bait Tanks

The bait tank is what turns bait you bought or made into bait you can still fish
hours later. A good tank keeps a **clean, circular flow** with enough turnover for
its volume, and doesn't beat up or crowd the bait. Get bait into the tank per
[making bait](making-bait.md); fish what survives per
[fishing live bait](fishing-live-bait.md).

## West-coast vs. east-coast tank design (1QWstxUibDA)

The two coasts build bait tanks differently, and the **west-coast round/oval
circular-flow tank is strongly preferred for SoCal live-bait fishing** (1QWstxUibDA):

- **East-coast style:** a **standpipe / drain tube in the middle** of the tank,
  screwed into the bottom; water drains out the top of the tube once the level
  rises to it. The problem — **anchovy and sardine get their noses stuck in the
  standpipe holes, bleed out, and die.**
- **West-coast style:** a **round tank with circular water flow.** Incoming water
  is injected around the tank so the **whole water column is moving**, and the
  bait swims **with the current** (either direction). A **side drain** sets the
  water level (lower it or raise it by opening the port). Nothing for the bait to
  jam its nose into, so **anchovy and sardine last much longer.**

The takeaways: **round/oval, not square**; **circular flow, not a center
standpipe**; keep the **whole column turning**; and set the drain so the bait
isn't fighting to stay off a screen.

## Sizing flow to volume (GPH-to-gallons)

A bait pump is rated in **GPH (gallons per hour)**. Match the pump's GPH to the
tank's gallons so the water **turns over enough times per hour** to stay
oxygenated without blasting the bait. A pump moving many times the tank's volume
per hour keeps the column alive and circular; too little flow and the bait uses up
the oxygen and dies.

## Keeping it clean and not overloading

- **Don't overload the tank** — too many baits for the volume crashes the oxygen
  and fouls the water. Bait density has to sit within what the flow can support.
- **Keep it clean** — cull dead and dying baits promptly (the bloody, scaly,
  lethargic ones) so they don't foul the tank; they go to the chum bucket, not
  back in the water column (see bait selection in
  [fishing live bait](fishing-live-bait.md)).
- **Steady flow** is the whole job — an interruption to the pump is what kills a
  tank of bait.

## Worked example — Cameron's panga tank

Cameron's rig on the [Panga Marine Marquesas 22](../profiles/cameron/boat.md).
The tank is dialed — bait normally survives well in it. All attributed to Cameron:

- **Tank:** 30 gallons.
- **Pump:** **Rule 401 Tournament Series, 500 GPH, 12V, 2.1 A draw, 3 A fuse.**
- **Power:** the panga has **no AC / shore-power inlet and no house battery** — the
  bait tank runs off the **single starting battery**. A **NOCO** onboard marine
  charger holds the battery up so the **bait pump can run overnight** without
  killing the start battery.
- **Draining:** he drains the 30-gal tank coming in — that's heavy stern water on
  a small foam-filled hull (trim/ballast detail in
  [boat](../profiles/cameron/boat.md)).

When Cameron makes bait it's mackerel; when the barge has them he buys sardine —
see [making bait](making-bait.md).
